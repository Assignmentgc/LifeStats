# LifeStats: make everyday progress visible

**Audience:** People interested in practical personal growth  
**Length:** 5-minute presentation, followed by a 4-minute guided demo

---

## Slide 1 — Progress is hard to see

**On the slide**

- We set goals, build routines, and make small choices every day.
- But it is easy to forget what is working—or where our attention is going.
- LifeStats gives those everyday efforts a place to add up.

**Say**

“LifeStats is a personal progress companion. It helps you reflect on your day, notice patterns, and keep the next small step in view. It is not about being perfect or competing with anyone.”

---

## Slide 2 — A quick check-in turns a day into a useful reflection

**On the slide**

- Describe what you actually did, felt, or learned.
- Type, or use supported on-device voice input.
- Review your words before choosing whether to submit.
- Eligible evidence can update relevant stats, within daily limits.

**Say**

“Instead of filling out a long survey, you can write a few sentences in your own words. The check-in is a prompt to reflect—not a test. LifeStats looks for concrete evidence of actions, and it does not award points for plans, uncertain claims, or negative experiences.”

---

## Slide 3 — See the bigger picture, without losing the detail

**On the slide**

- Six broad areas: Purpose, Vitality, Discipline, Social, Finances, and Environment.
- Each area can be explored through more specific substats.
- A LifeScore and progress views help summarize change over time.

**Say**

“The overview gives you a quick snapshot. The detail view lets you ask better questions: ‘Am I making time for the things I value?’ or ‘What routines have I been following?’ Think of the scores as reflection aids—not grades, diagnoses, or objective measures of a person.”

---

## Slide 4 — Turn intention into a repeatable next step

**On the slide**

- **Habits:** keep a streak and earn experience for completing a daily habit.
- **To-Do:** organize next actions by what deserves attention.
- **Journal:** save a reflection you can revisit later.

**Say**

“LifeStats connects reflection with action. Habits reward consistency, To-Do helps reduce the ‘what should I do next?’ feeling, and the journal keeps your own story in one place. You can use the pieces that are helpful to you.”

---

## Slide 5 — Personalization and thoughtful controls

**On the slide**

- Choose light or dark appearance.
- Spirituality tracking is optional and configurable.
- Microphone recognition, when supported, runs on-device; LifeStats does not upload microphone audio.
- Sending check-in text for analysis requires your explicit consent.

**Say**

“You stay in control of whether to submit a check-in. If you use voice, LifeStats uses supported on-device recognition only; if the device cannot do that, you can type instead. Before analysis, the app asks permission to send the written text and limited check-in context to its analysis provider. Read the app’s privacy notice and provider terms before using real personal information.”

---

## Slide 6 — The benefit: a clearer feedback loop

**On the slide**

1. **Notice** what happened.
2. **Reflect** on what mattered.
3. **Choose** a small next step.
4. **Look back** and learn from the pattern.

**Say**

“The practical benefit is a simple feedback loop: notice your effort, decide what to do next, and look back to see what is changing. You do not need a perfect streak or a high score for the reflection to be useful.”

---

# Guided demo — about 4 minutes

## Before the demo

- Start on **Home** (`/dashboard`) and use a dedicated demo account with synthetic data if you want to save anything.
- The signed-in browser may show real personal stats or journal content. Do not project or read private content without the account owner's permission.
- For a no-write walkthrough, describe the sample check-in below but **do not select consent or save it**. Saving sends text for analysis and stores evidence in the account; completing a habit also changes account data.
- Avoid presenting the LifeScore as a health, clinical, or objective assessment.

## 1. Home — the one-screen overview (30–45 seconds)

**Show:** LifeScore, the six character stats, level/XP progress, and the To-Do shortcut.

**Say:** “This is the quick overview. It brings the main signals together so you can decide where to look next. The number is a personal progress summary, not a verdict.”

**Do:** Point out where a user would open a check-in and where the detailed stats live. Do not read out a real account's values.

## 2. Check-In — turn a day into a reflection (60–90 seconds)

**Show:** Select **Check-In**. Point out the text box, editable transcript, optional on-device microphone, and consent checkbox.

**Say:** “A useful entry is specific and honest. For example: ‘I took a 20-minute walk after lunch, cooked dinner at home, and called a friend I had been meaning to catch up with.’ You can edit the transcript before deciding whether to submit.”

**Explain:** Submitting check-in text is optional and requires consent. The response can show detected evidence and any eligible changes. Check-ins award no XP; their gains are capped per substat per day. No-write demo: keep the example verbal and leave the form untouched.

## 3. Stats — explore what the summary means (45–60 seconds)

**Show:** Select **Stats**. Point out the six broad stats, their component substats, and the monthly-progress chart.

**Say:** “The detail helps turn a broad feeling like ‘I want more balance’ into areas you can reflect on. Trends need time and entries to become meaningful; a sparse or early chart is normal.”

## 4. Habits — make a small routine visible (30–45 seconds)

**Show:** Select **Habits**. Point out streak length and the daily completion action.

**Say:** “Habits are the repeatable actions. Completing one protects its streak and earns XP. The check-in and habit systems have different roles: check-ins capture reflection; completed daily habits earn XP.”

**Do:** Do not press **Complete** on a real account during a demo.

## 5. Journal, To-Do, and settings — choose your supports (45–60 seconds)

**Show:** Briefly point to **Journal** for free-form entries, **To-Do** for categorized next actions, and **Settings** for theme and optional spirituality tracking.

**Say:** “You can keep a private written reflection, make the next action concrete, and adjust a couple of preferences. Start small; the app is most useful when it fits your routine.”

## Close with this prompt

“What is one small thing you would like to notice or make easier this week?”
